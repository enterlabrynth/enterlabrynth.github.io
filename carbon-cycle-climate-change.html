Carbon Cycle & Climate Change

How carbon moves between the air, living things and rock, and what happens when we shift that balance. Part 1 is the carbon cycle. Part 2 is the evidence that the climate is changing now. Part 3 is the evidence that we are the cause.

STEP 1 · START HERE

One atom, two jobs

Carbon is the atom every organic molecule is built on. In the air, as CO₂, it is also a gas that traps heat. So the cycle that feeds every food web also helps set the planet's temperature. That is why the carbon cycle and climate change belong together.

THE REACTION THAT TAKES CARBON OUT OF THE AIR

Photosynthesis

INGREDIENTSCO₂+H₂O
PRODUCTS"CH₂O"+O₂
Counting matter only. The energy comes from sunlight.

Turning inorganic CO₂ into organic molecules is carbon fixation. Autotrophs fix carbon: plants, algae and cyanobacteria by photosynthesis, and chemoautotrophs without light.

THE REACTION THAT PUTS IT BACK

Cellular respiration

INGREDIENTS"CH₂O"+O₂
PRODUCTSCO₂+H₂O
The same molecules, in reverse. Every living cell does this, producers included.

The two reactions are mirror images. One removes CO₂ from the air and the other returns it.

Biogeochemical cycle

ONE WAY IN, THREE WAYS OUT

In and out of the air

TAKES CO₂ OUT OF THE AIRPhotosynthesis.
PUTS CO₂ BACKCellular respiration by producers, by consumers, and by the microbes that decompose detritus.
COMPARE WITH NITROGEN A nitrogen atom in an animal had to wait for death or excretion, then decomposition, to become usable again. A carbon atom has a shortcut: the animal can simply breathe it out as CO₂.
KEY TERMS · TAP ONE TO OPEN IT

Dead matter and waste.

Decomposition

The breakdown of detritus by microbes. Their respiration returns its carbon to the air or water as CO₂.

Flux

The movement of an element from one pool to another, as a rate.

THE METHOD

Arrows in, minus arrows out

1Pick one pool
The atmosphere, the land, the ocean. You are doing the accounts for that pool only.
2Add up every arrow pointing IN
Each arrow on the diagram is a flux. Total the ones that bring carbon into your pool.
3Add up every arrow pointing OUT
Total the fluxes that carry carbon away from it.
4Subtract
Net flux = fluxes in − fluxes out.
READING THE ANSWER

What the sign tells you

POSITIVEMore comes in than leaves. The pool is growing.
ZEROIn and out balance. The pool stays the same size, however big the fluxes are.
NEGATIVEMore leaves than comes in. The pool is shrinking.

Always give the size, the units (gigatons of carbon per year) and the direction: for the atmosphere, "a net flux of about 6 gigatons a year, into the atmosphere".

Photosynthesis and respiration are enormous but balanced, so alone they leave the atmosphere about where it was. Fossil fuel burning has nothing balancing it. It moves carbon that was locked in rock for millions of years into the air, and it only goes in one direction.

TWO TIMESCALES

Short-term vs. long-term

SHORT-TERM · YEARS TO DECADESBiological and local: photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition. Carbon moves between the air, water and living things.
LONG-TERM · MILLIONS OF YEARSGeological and global: carbon is buried in sediments, becomes rock (and fossil fuels), and returns slowly through volcanoes and weathering.
SUMMARY

How CO₂ is added to and removed from the air

ADDED BYRespiration, including decomposition. Volcanoes. Burning fossil fuels. Land-use change such as clearing forests.
REMOVED BYPhotosynthesis. Dissolving into the ocean. Chemical weathering of rock.

WHY THE NORTH SETS THE RHYTHM

Most of the land is in the Northern Hemisphere

Most of Earth's land, and so most of its land plants, is north of the equator. That includes the taiga, the huge belt of forest around 60° north. When the north has its summer, global photosynthesis peaks, and the whole atmosphere shows it.

DOES CO₂ STAY WHERE IT IS RELEASED?

No. It travels

Winds carry CO₂ away from its sources and mix it through the atmosphere. Stations all over the world record the same pattern as Mauna Loa. So CO₂ released in one country changes the air over every country.

Taiga

The belt of cold northern forest that runs across North America, Europe and Asia at around 60° north.

CARBON VS. NITROGEN

Why carbon's path is shorter

A CARBON ATOMSquid → respiration → CO₂ → photosynthesis → eaten. Three moves.
A NITROGEN ATOMSeal → death or waste → detritus → decomposition → ammonium → uptake → eaten, eaten. Five moves.

Every organism releases carbon all the time as CO₂. Nitrogen only becomes available to producers again after decomposers have worked on it.

Lock it in · Part 1

  1. Photosynthesis fixes CO₂ into organic molecules. Cellular respiration, by every organism, returns it.
  2. A plant's carbon comes from the air. Its nitrogen and phosphorus come from the soil.
  3. Carbon reaches consumers by consumption, digestion and assimilation. Decomposers return the carbon in detritus as CO₂.
  4. The largest carbon pool is rock and sediment. The largest fluxes are photosynthesis and respiration.
  5. Natural fluxes nearly balance. Fossil fuel burning is small but one-way, so the net flux is into the atmosphere.
  6. CO₂ falls each northern summer (photosynthesis) and rises each winter (respiration), while the yearly average keeps climbing.

STEP 1 · TWO WORDS PEOPLE MIX UP

Weather is not climate

A cold week does not disprove a warming planet, and a hot one does not prove it. To see why, separate two things that are measured on very different clocks.

DIAGRAM · THE SAME MEASUREMENT ON TWO TIMESCALES

The wiggle and the trend

The difference between weather and climate is the time interval. Weather is what is happening this hour or this week. Climate is the pattern over decades or longer.

DEFINITIONS

Weather, climate, climate change

WEATHERThe state of the atmosphere over a short time.
CLIMATEThe pattern of weather over an extended period: decades or longer.
CLIMATE CHANGEAny significant change in measures of climate (temperature, precipitation, humidity, wind) that lasts for an extended period: decades or longer.
WHY "CLIMATE CHANGE" AND NOT JUST "GLOBAL WARMING" Because temperature is only one of the things changing. Rain and snow, humidity, wind, sea level, ocean chemistry and the frequency of extreme events are all shifting too, and not every place is changing in the same way.
KEY TERMS · TAP ONE TO OPEN IT
Weather

The state of the atmosphere over a short time: hours, days or weeks.

Climate

The long-term pattern of weather, averaged over decades or longer.

WHAT THE MAPS ARE FOR

An anomaly map is the average, taken apart

The global figure of 1.1 °C is an average. A map shows what is hiding inside it: every place has its own anomaly. Some are far above the average, some are near zero, and a few are below it.

So is the whole United States, or the whole planet, experiencing the same thing? No. The impacts people are living with right now depend on where they are.

WHY HAVE A FEW PLACES NOT WARMED?

Pollution can hide warming

One reason some areas have warmed little, or even cooled slightly: smog. Haze and particle pollution in the air block some of the sun's energy before it reaches the ground, which cools the surface underneath.

That is not good news. It is one kind of pollution masking the effect of another. The greenhouse gases are still there, and when the air is cleaned up, as it should be for people's health, the hidden warming shows.

WHY IS THE SEA RISING?

Two causes, both from warming

ICE IS MELTINGThe polar ice caps and glaciers are melting, and the meltwater from ice that sat on land runs into the ocean and adds to it.
WATER EXPANDS WHEN HEATEDThe ocean has absorbed most of the extra heat. Warmer water takes up more room, so the same amount of water stands higher.
HOW THE TWO GRAPHS ARE RELATED

Same cause, opposite directions

Both are driven by a warming planet. As temperature goes up, ice extent goes down and sea level goes up.

One detail worth knowing: sea ice is already floating, so when it melts it barely changes sea level, like ice cubes melting in a full glass. The rise comes from ice on land (Greenland, Antarctica, mountain glaciers) and from warm water expanding. Shrinking sea ice is the visible sign of the same warming.

THE CHEMISTRY

Why dissolved CO₂ makes water acidic

CO₂ + H₂OH₂CO₃ · carbonic acidH⁺ + HCO₃⁻
HOW MUCHAbout 30% of atmospheric CO₂ dissolves into ocean water.
WHAT IT MAKESCO₂ reacts with water to form carbonic acid, which releases hydrogen ions (H⁺).
WHAT pH MEASURESThe concentration of H⁺. More H⁺ means a lower pH, and a lower pH means more acidic.
WHY A SMALL NUMBER IS A BIG CHANGE The drop on the graph looks tiny: a few hundredths of a pH unit. But the pH scale is not a simple count. Each step down is a large jump in H⁺, so a small fall in pH is a real change in the water's chemistry.

Lock it in · Part 2

  1. Weather is short-term. Climate is the pattern over decades or longer. The difference is the time interval.
  2. Global average temperature is up about 2 °F (1.1 °C) in 100 years, most of it since 1975.
  3. An anomaly is the difference from a long-term average. Some areas are warming more rapidly than others, and smog can mask warming in a few.
  4. Hurricanes have more energy (warmer water), more rain (more water vapor) and a bigger storm surge (higher sea level).
  5. Sea level is rising because ice is melting and water expands when heated. Arctic sea ice is shrinking.
  6. About 30% of atmospheric CO₂ dissolves in the ocean: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. pH falls, which harms shell and skeleton builders.
  7. Heat waves are up in frequency, duration, season length and intensity.
  8. Air holds about 4% more water vapor per 1 °F, which brings drought to some places and heavier rain and floods to others.

HOW IT WORKS

Four steps from snowfall to a CO₂ reading

1Snow falls and piles up

DEFINITION

What is land-use change?

Land-use change is any change in how people use an area of land that changes how much carbon it holds. The big one is clearing or burning forest to make farmland.

WHY IT ADDS CO₂A forest is a store of carbon. Cut and burned, or left to rot, the carbon in the wood is oxidized to CO₂.
AND IT REMOVES A SINKThe trees that were taking CO₂ out of the air by photosynthesis are no longer there to do it.
IT CAN RUN BACKWARDWhen cleared land is left to regrow, the new forest takes carbon back. That is the smaller "regrowth" arrow in the net flux sums in Part 1.
HOW FOSSIL FUELS FORM

Detritus that never got decomposed

1Plants and algae die
Their bodies become detritus, full of carbon they fixed from the air.
2Decomposition fails
Not all detritus is recycled. Decomposers are especially inefficient where oxygen is low, as in bogs and swamps.
3Burial
The undecomposed detritus piles up, is buried under sediment, and is fossilized over millions of years.
4Coal, oil, natural gas
What is left is made of hydrocarbons: molecules of carbon and hydrogen, packed with chemical energy.

THE VERDICT

The extra CO₂ was once plants

The air's δ¹³C has declined over time, which means the share of ¹²C is going up. The CO₂ being added must be low in ¹³C.

That rules sources in and out. CO₂ from volcanoes has too much ¹³C to fit. CO₂ from the ocean is too close to the air to move it. Only carbon that came through photosynthesis fits: fossil fuels, plus the forests cleared by land-use change.

A third, radioactive isotope (¹⁴C) separates those last two. Living plants contain ¹⁴C and fossil fuels contain none, and the air's ¹⁴C share has been falling too. So most of the added carbon is fossil.

WHAT THE AIR IS MADE OF

CO₂ is a tiny share, with a big effect

Nitrogen (N₂) · about 78%
Oxygen (O₂) · about 21%
Carbon dioxide · about 0.04%

Nitrogen and oxygen make up almost all of the air, and neither is a greenhouse gas. CO₂ is not one of the main components. It matters because of what it does: it absorbs heat and radiates it back. Going from 0.03% to 0.04% is a one-third increase in the gas doing that job.

TWO THINGS IT IS NOT

Clear these up now

NOT THE SAME AS CLIMATE CHANGEThe greenhouse effect is the natural process. Climate change is what happens when we strengthen it by adding greenhouse gases.
NOT THE OZONE HOLEThe ozone layer blocks ultraviolet light. A hole in it is a separate problem. The greenhouse effect is about heat going out, not extra sunlight coming in.
SOME IS GOOD. TOO MUCH IS THE PROBLEM. We need greenhouse gases. They hold in some of the heat the surface gives off, and without them Earth would be about 60 °F colder. The trouble is not that they exist. It is that we keep adding to them, so more heat is held in than before.

WHY MODELS

An experiment you cannot run for real

THE PROBLEMNo control planet. We cannot remove human emissions from Earth and watch what happens.
WHAT A MODEL DOESSimulates the climate from physics, with causes switched on or off, so their effects can be separated.
HOW TO JUDGE ONECompare it with observations. A model that matches the record we have earns trust for the future it projects.
LOOKING FORWARD

The same models predict the future

Once a model reproduces the past, scientists run it forward under different choices: emissions that keep rising, or emissions that fall. That is where predictions such as "more than 2 °C by 2100" come from. Step 8 uses two of them.

STEP 7 · DO SCIENTISTS AGREE?

A political debate, a scientific consensus

There is political debate about climate change. Among the scientists who study it, there is very strong agreement, and that agreement has itself been measured, more than once.

DATA · 100 DOTS FOR 100 PAPERS

Over 99% of climate research papers

more than 99 in every 100 papers

A 2021 study went through 88,125 climate papers published since 2012 and found that more than 99% conclude that humans are changing the climate.

Lynas, Houlton and Perry, 2021, Environmental Research Letters.

THE STUDIES BEHIND THE NUMBER

Checked again and again, by different methods

2004 · ORESKESRead 928 research abstracts on global climate change. None rejected the consensus position.
2009 · DORAN AND ZIMMERMANSurveyed earth scientists. About 97% of actively publishing climate specialists agreed humans are a significant cause.
2013 · COOK AND COLLEAGUESSorted about 12,000 abstracts. Of those that took a position, about 97% endorsed human-caused warming.
2016 · COOK AND COLLEAGUESThe paper about the papers. It compared the separate consensus studies and found they agree with each other: 90 to 100% of publishing climate scientists.
2021 · LYNAS AND COLLEAGUESThe largest count so far: more than 99% of papers.
WHAT CONSENSUS MEANS HERE Not a vote, and not an opinion poll. It is what thousands of independent studies, using different data and methods, keep concluding. The political argument is about what to do. The scientific question of whether humans are changing the climate is settled.
KEY TERMS · TAP ONE TO OPEN IT
Scientific consensus

The position that the great majority of scientists working in a field have reached from the evidence. On human-caused climate change it is more than 99% of published papers.

STEP 8 · WHAT HAS TO HAPPEN

Emissions have to fall to zero

If we know it is real, and we know we are causing it, the way to slow it follows directly: stop adding CO₂.

The dashed line is a sketch of the direction needed, not a published pathway.

WHERE THE GRAPH COMES FROM

The Global Carbon Budget

A report published every year by an international team of scientists. It adds up every source of CO₂ (fossil fuels, land-use change) and every place it ends up (the air, the ocean, the land).

It is the same sum as the net flux in Part 1, arrows in minus arrows out, worked out fresh for the whole planet each year.

WHY EMISSIONS ARE STILL RISING

Demand for energy is outrunning clean energy

Renewable energy is growing quickly. But the world's demand for energy is growing too, and renewables do not yet cover it, so fossil fuels are burned to fill the gap. Efforts to decarbonise are not yet strong enough to overcome growing global energy needs.

The goal: get to zero CO₂ emissions as fast as possible, by moving to 100% renewable energy.

IS IT TOO LATE?

No. The window to act is still open

We are not past the point of no return, but the window will not stay open for long. The sooner we act, the lower the risk and the cost.

Every bit of warming matters, every year matters, and every choice matters.

The best way to slow climate change: stop adding CO₂. That means rapidly cutting the burning of fossil fuels to zero. Module 2.6 covers how.

Lock it in · Part 3

  1. CO₂ was about 280 ppm for thousands of years, never above 300 in 800,000 years, and is now about 427. Past levels come from air bubbles trapped in ice cores.
  2. The extra comes from burning fossil fuels (about 80%) and land-use change: clearing and burning forest.
  3. Fossil fuels are detritus that was buried before it decomposed, in low-oxygen places such as swamps. They are made of hydrocarbons.
  4. Most carbon is ¹²C. The heavier ¹³C diffuses through the stomata more slowly and the carbon-fixing enzyme prefers ¹²C, so plants, and fossil fuels, are low in ¹³C.
  5. δ¹³C is how far a sample's ¹³C : ¹²C ratio is from a standard, in per mil (‰). Photosynthesis pushes the air's δ¹³C up. Burning plant carbon pushes it down.
  6. Photosynthesis and respiration balance, and so do the ocean fluxes. Fossil fuel burning does not. As CO₂ has risen, the air's δ¹³C has fallen.
  7. Greenhouse effect: the surface radiates heat, and greenhouse gases absorb it and send some back. Some is essential. More gas traps more heat. It is not the ozone hole.
  8. Models match the temperature record only when human causes are included.
  9. More than 99% of climate research papers conclude that humans are changing the climate.
  10. To stay under the 2 °C limit, emissions must fall rapidly to zero.
Vocab · 32 terms
Biogeochemical cycle The path an element takes as it moves from one pool to another.
Pool A place where an element is held. Also called a reservoir.
Reservoir Another word for a pool.
Flux The movement of an element from one pool to another, as a rate.
Biosphere All of the living things on Earth, taken together.
Carbon dioxide CO₂. The inorganic form of carbon in the air and dissolved in water, and a greenhouse gas.
Fixation Carbon fixation is turning inorganic carbon (CO₂) into organic molecules. Autotrophs do it.
Detritus Dead matter and waste.
Decomposition The breakdown of detritus by microbes. Their respiration returns its carbon to the air or water as CO₂.
Sequester To lock something away. Carbon is sequestered when it is stored somewhere it will not return to the air for a long time.
Taiga The belt of cold northern forest that runs across North America, Europe and Asia at around 60° north.
Weather The state of the atmosphere over a short time: hours, days or weeks.
Climate The long-term pattern of weather, averaged over decades or longer.
Anomaly How far a measurement is from a long-term average. A temperature anomaly of +1 °C means 1 °C warmer than that average.
pH A measure of how acidic a solution is. More hydrogen ions (H⁺) means a lower pH and a more acidic solution.
Ice core A long cylinder of ice drilled out of an ice sheet. Bubbles trapped in each layer hold a sample of the air from the year that layer formed.
Land-use change A change in how people use an area of land that changes how much carbon it holds. Most often: clearing or burning forest for farmland.
Fossil fuel Coal, oil and natural gas. Formed from ancient detritus that was buried before it could be decomposed.
Hydrocarbon A molecule made of carbon and hydrogen. Fossil fuels are made of hydrocarbons: oil and natural gas almost entirely, while coal is the most carbon-rich of the three.
Isotope Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons, and so different masses.
¹²C (carbon 12) The common, lighter isotope of carbon. About 98.9% of all carbon.
¹³C (carbon 13) The rarer, heavier isotope of carbon. About 1.1% of all carbon.
Stomata Tiny pores in the surface of a leaf (one is a stoma; also called stomates). CO₂ diffuses in through them, and water vapor and oxygen diffuse out.
Isotopic signature The characteristic ratio of isotopes in a material. It works like a fingerprint for where the material's atoms came from.
δ¹³C How far a sample's ratio of ¹³C to ¹²C is from a standard, in parts per thousand (per mil, ‰). Lower means relatively less ¹³C.
Per mil (‰) Parts per thousand. Per cent (%) is parts per hundred. Per mil is ten times finer, which suits very small differences.
Greenhouse gas A gas that absorbs heat radiated by Earth's surface and re-emits some of it back down. CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide and water vapor are greenhouse gases.
Greenhouse effect The warming of Earth's surface caused by greenhouse gases trapping heat.
Methane (CH₄) A greenhouse gas. Its concentration has risen by about 148%.
Nitrous oxide (N₂O) A greenhouse gas. Its concentration has risen by about 18%.
Scientific consensus The position that the great majority of scientists working in a field have reached from the evidence. On human-caused climate change it is more than 99% of published papers.
