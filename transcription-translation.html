MODULE 2.4 · PART 1

DNA → RNA

THE QUESTIONA gene sits in the DNA, in the nucleus. The protein it describes is built by a ribosome, outside. How does the message get out?

Six steps: the journey from gene to protein, DNA vs. RNA, reading a gene, transcription start to finish, RNA processing, and replication vs. transcription.

STEP 2 · TRANSCRIPTION

DNA is the master copy. RNA is the working copy.

Before a cell can build a protein, it copies the gene into RNA. Here's what's different about the copy.

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
WHY MAKE A COPY?DNA stays put; RNA travelsThe cell doesn't send its DNA to the ribosomes. It makes an RNA copy of one gene, and the ribosome reads that. DNA and RNA use the same language of nucleic acids, so copying one into the other is just base pairing.
THE STRANDSTwo strands vs. oneDNA is a double helix. An RNA copy is a single strand, made from just one of DNA's strands.
THE SUGAROne oxygen apartRNA's sugar is ribose, with an OH on the 2′ carbon. DNA's is deoxyribose, with just an H there ("de-oxy": one oxygen fewer). Both have up to three phosphates on the 5′ carbon and an OH on the 3′ carbon.
THE BASET in DNA, U in RNADNA's bases: G, A, T, C. RNA's: G, A, U, C. Uracil is thymine without its methyl group, and it pairs with A just like T does.Building blocks: dNTPs (deoxynucleoside triphosphates) for DNA, NTPs for RNA.
STEP 3 · TRANSCRIPTION

Reading a gene: one strand is the template

Our example gene, every letter shown. Which strand does RNA polymerase read, and what does the RNA say?

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
1 · OUR GENETwo antiparallel strandsHere's the example gene this module follows. It starts with a promoter (with a TATA box), then the region that gets copied. The two DNA strands run in opposite directions.
2 · ONE STRAND IS THE TEMPLATEOnly one strand is readDuring transcription, one strand of DNA is used as the template. RNA polymerase reads it 3′ → 5′. The other strand is the non-template strand.
3 · THE RNA PAIRS WITH ITComplementary, antiparallel, 5′ → 3′Each new RNA nucleotide pairs with the template: A–U (RNA has no T), T–A, G–C, C–G. The RNA is antiparallel to the template and grows 5′ → 3′.
4 · A COPY OF THE CODING STRANDSame letters, U for TRead the RNA against the top strand: it's the same sequence, with U in place of every T. That's why the non-template strand is also called the coding strand.

Which strand? It depends on the gene

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
TWO GENES, ONE STRETCH OF DNAGenes can be on either strandHere are two genes side by side. The arrows show which way each gene's RNA is made. For Gene A, which strand is the template?
GENE AIts RNA grows to the right: the bottom strandRNA grows 5′ → 3′ and is antiparallel to its template, so the template is read 3′ → 5′ in the direction the RNA grows. Going right, that's the bottom strand.
GENE BIts RNA grows to the left: the top strandGoing left, the strand that runs 3′ → 5′ is the top one. Same DNA, a different gene, so a different template strand. The rule didn't change.
THE RULEBoth strands can be templates. Each gene uses one.Which strand is the template depends on where the gene's promoter is and which way transcription goes from it.

Practice: write the RNA

PRACTICEWrite the RNARNA polymerase transcribes this stretch of DNA, and the top strand is the template. What's the RNA? Try it before you tap through.
1 · PAIR WITH THE TEMPLATEComplementary, with U for AUnder each template base, write its partner: G–C, C–G, T–A and A–U. Antiparallel, so the RNA runs 3′ → 5′ under a 5′ → 3′ template.
2 · CHECKSame as the other strand, with UThe RNA matches the non-template strand letter for letter, except U where the DNA has T.
3 · WRITE IT 5′ → 3′Answer dSequences are written 5′ → 3′, so read the RNA backwards: 5′–ACGAUUCGCAUC–3′. Put 5′ and 3′ on every strand before you answer.
TEMPLATE VS. CODING
Template strand: the one RNA polymerase reads and pairs with. Coding (non-template) strand: has the same sequence as the RNA, with T instead of U.
STEP 4 · TRANSCRIPTION

Transcription, start to finish

Watch our gene get copied, letter by letter. Then zoom in on each stage.

1 · INITIATIONTranscription factors find the promoter; RNA polymerase binds and unwinds the DNA.
2 · ELONGATIONRNA polymerase reads the template 3′ → 5′ and builds RNA 5′ → 3′.
3 · TERMINATIONAt the terminator, a hairpin in the RNA pulls its 3′ end away from RNA polymerase: the termination signal. The polymerase falls off and the RNA is released.

Zoom 1 · Initiation: how RNA polymerase finds the start

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 3
(a) THE PROMOTERWhere a gene startsA promoter is a DNA sequence just "before" the region to be transcribed. Many eukaryotic promoters have a TATA box. Transcription starts at the startpoint and ends at a terminator.
(b) TRANSCRIPTION FACTORSProteins that recognize the promoterTranscription factors recognize and bind the promoter, then recruit the RNA polymerase enzyme.
(c) THE INITIATION COMPLEXRNA polymerase binds, unwinds, startsTranscription factors and RNA polymerase bind the double-stranded DNA at the promoter. The DNA is unwound and RNA synthesis begins: no primer needed.

Zoom 2 · Elongation: one new bond

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 3
1 · BASE PAIRING DECIDESOnly the right nucleotide fitsIncoming ribonucleotides (NTPs) are accepted if they correctly base pair with the template DNA. Opposite an A, RNA puts a U.
2 · ADDED TO THE 3′ ENDThe 3′ OH bonds to the new nucleotideThe 3′ OH at the end of the growing RNA bonds to the incoming nucleotide's first phosphate, breaking the high-energy bond between its phosphates. New nucleotides only go on the 3′ end, so RNA grows 5′ → 3′.
3 · THE ENERGYFrom the nucleotide's own phosphatesTwo of the incoming nucleotide's three phosphates leave as pyrophosphate; that provides the energy for the new bond, the same as in DNA replication.

Zoom 3 · Termination: the hairpin

WHAT RNA POLYMERASE NEEDS
A promoter, a template strand, the four NTPs (ATP, GTP, CTP, UTP) and separated DNA strands. Not a primer: DNA polymerase always needs one, RNA polymerase never does.
WHAT IF THE PROMOTER IS MUTATED?
A mutation in the promoter can stop transcription factors binding: the coding sequence is perfect, but no RNA means no protein. A mutation in the coding region still gives the same amount of mRNA; it may change the protein.
WHAT IF A NUCLEOTIDE HAS NO 3′ OH?
A nucleotide analog missing its 3′ OH can be added to the chain, but nothing can be added after it: the chain stops.
WHY RNA MISTAKES MATTER LESS
RNA polymerase makes more errors than DNA polymerase, but they matter less: a bad RNA is one of many copies, it doesn't last, and it isn't passed on. A DNA error is permanent and inherited.
STEP 5 · AFTER TRANSCRIPTION · EUKARYOTES

RNA processing: cap, tail, splice

Prokaryotes use their RNA in translation right away. Eukaryotes finish it first. Watch our gene's RNA get processed.

Why the cap and tail?

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 2
UNPROTECTEDExonucleases eat RNA from the endsExonucleases are enzymes that chew a nucleic acid from its ends. A bare RNA wouldn't last long enough to be translated.
CAPPED AND TAILEDProtection, and a signalThe 5′ cap (a unique 5′–5′ bond) and the poly(A) tail protect the mRNA from exonucleases. The cap is also how the ribosome recognizes the mRNA, and the tail helps target it for transport to the cytoplasm.

The finished mRNA, letter by letter

COMMON MIX-UP
Exons are kept and expressed; introns are taken out. The UTRs are part of the exons: they stay in the mRNA but aren't translated.
WHAT IF AN INTRON STAYS IN?
If an intron is not removed, its bases stay in the mRNA and get read as codons: extra amino acids, or a shifted reading frame that often hits an early stop codon.
WHY IS A GENE LONGER THAN ITS mRNA?
A gene's DNA is longer than its mature mRNA because of the introns. The mRNA is longer than 3 × the number of amino acids because of the UTRs, the stop codon, the cap and the tail.

Reading frames: where you start matters

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 3
THE START CODON SETS THE FRAMEOur gene, read from AUGThe ribosome starts at the start codon AUG and reads three letters at a time: AUG GAA CGC CUC, then the stop codon UAA. That's Met, Glu, Arg, Leu.
ONE LETTER OFFA different proteinStart one letter later and every codon changes: UGG AAC GCC UCU… That's why the start codon matters so much, and why adding or removing one base is so damaging (next module).
NON-OVERLAPPING, CONTINUOUSEach letter is used onceCodons are read one after another: no letter belongs to two codons, and there are no gaps between them.

Practice: the anticodon

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
PRACTICEMutation, then the anticodonRefer to the mRNA 5′–AUGAGACUUACCGAA–3′. If the DNA were mutated so the second nucleotide of the fourth codon became U, what would the matching anticodon look like?
1 · FIND IT, CHANGE ITACC → AUCCodons from the 5′ end: AUG · AGA · CUU · ACC · GAA. Its second nucleotide becomes U: AUC (Ile instead of Thr).
2 · PAIR THE ANTICODONComplementary and antiparallelUnder 5′–AUC–3′ the anticodon reads 3′–UAG–5′ (A–U, U–A, C–G). It's RNA, so no T.
3 · THE ANSWERD. 3′–UAG–5′Traps: a T means DNA, and an anticodon is never the same letters as the codon.
FEATURES OF THE CODE
Triplet: three bases per codon. No ambiguity: each codon means one amino acid. Redundancy: most amino acids have several codons. Start: AUG (Met). Three stops: UAA, UAG, UGA. Non-overlapping and continuous. Highly conserved: nearly the same in all living things.
COMMON MIX-UP
The codon table is read on the mRNA, 5′ → 3′. Given DNA, or a sequence written 3′ → 5′, convert to mRNA 5′ → 3′ first.
STEP 2 · TRANSLATION

The translation crew

Who does what. Then: how a tRNA finds its codon, and how it gets its amino acid in the first place.

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 6
TRANSLATION TAKES A CREWThree kinds of RNA, plus helper proteinsTranslation needs mRNA, tRNA and rRNA (in ribosomes), each made by transcription from its own gene, plus proteins: aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and initiation, elongation and release factors.
mRNA · THE MESSAGESays which amino acids, in which orderThe mRNA specifies the sequence of amino acids, written in codons.
tRNA · THE ADAPTERSpeaks both languagesA tRNA links the mRNA's nucleic-acid language to amino acids: its anticodon pairs with a codon, and its 3′ end carries the matching amino acid. Short RNAs that fold on themselves: a cloverleaf in 2D, an upside-down L in 3D.
THE RIBOSOME · THE MACHINErRNA + protein, two subunitsA large and a small subunit, about 60% rRNA and 40% protein. It holds the mRNA and the tRNAs and joins the amino acids.
SYNTHETASE · THE LOADERPuts the right amino acid on each tRNAAminoacyl-tRNA synthetases attach specific amino acids to specific tRNAs, using ATP. Each one handles one amino acid.
RELEASE FACTOR · THE ENDERA protein, not a tRNAAt a stop codon, a release factor binds the A site and frees the finished polypeptide.

tRNAs read our codons

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
CODON 1 · AUGThe anticodon UACThe anticodon pairs with the codon base by base, antiparallel: under 5′–AUG–3′ sits 3′–UAC–5′. That tRNA carries Met.
CODON 2 · GAAThe anticodon CUUGAA pairs with CUU: the tRNA that carries Glu.
CODON 3 · CGCThe anticodon GCGCGC pairs with GCG: Arg.
CODON 4 · CUCThe anticodon GAGCUC pairs with GAG: Leu. Four codons, four tRNAs, four amino acids, in order.

Loading a tRNA with its amino acid

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
1 · BINDOne enzyme per amino acidHow does the right amino acid get on the right tRNA? An aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (sometimes called tRNA ligase). This one only handles leucine.
2 · ACTIVATEIt costs ATPATP becomes AMP, attached to the amino acid (an activated complex), and two phosphates leave.
3 · TRANSFEROnto the tRNA's 3′ endThe tRNA for leucine binds. Leucine is attached to its 3′ end, and the AMP leaves.
4 · RELEASEAn aminoacyl tRNAThe charged tRNA (an aminoacyl tRNA) is released, ready for translation.
WHAT THE SYNTHETASE NEEDS
The tRNA, the amino acid and ATP. Not the mRNA: tRNAs are loaded before they ever meet a ribosome.
COMMON MIX-UP
The codon is on the mRNA; the anticodon is on the tRNA. Complementary and antiparallel: codon 5′–UCA–3′, anticodon 3′–AGU–5′.

Translation, start to finish

2 · ELONGATIONArrive in A, peptide bond, move one codon. Repeat.

Zoom 2 · The ribosome's three sites

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 6
THE RIBOSOMEHolds the mRNA and up to three tRNAsThe mRNA runs between the two subunits, 5′ on the left. Three sites hold tRNAs: from the 5′ side, E, P, A.
TWO SUBUNITSLarge and smallEach made of ribosomal RNA and proteins: about 60% RNA, 40% protein.
P SITE · PEPTIDYLHolds the growing chainThe tRNA with the growing polypeptide attached. The peptide bond adds the next amino acid onto this chain.
A SITE · AMINOACYLAccepts the next tRNAAccepts the incoming aminoacyl tRNA whose anticodon matches the codon there.
E SITE · EXITEmpty tRNAs leaveA tRNA that has given up its amino acid exits from here.

Zoom 3 · Termination

Tap the buttons, or Next step, to click through all 4
1 · A STOP CODON IN THE A SITENo tRNA matches itProtein synthesis ends when the ribosome reaches a stop codon in the A site. A release factor, a protein, binds it.
2 · RELEASEDCut free from the last tRNAThe release factor cleaves the polypeptide from the tRNA in the P site, and the new polypeptide is released.
3 · COME APARTReady to start againThe ribosomal subunits and the other components dissociate.
ONE mRNA, MANY RIBOSOMESA polyribosomeA single mRNA can be translated many times, and several ribosomes can translate it at once: the further along (toward 3′), the longer the chain.
WHICH WAY?
The mRNA is read 5′ → 3′. The polypeptide grows from its amino (N) terminus to its carboxyl (C) terminus: Met, the first amino acid, is the N-terminus.
COMMON MIX-UP
Release factors are proteins, not tRNAs. No tRNA has an anticodon for a stop codon.
STEP 4 · THE BIG PICTURE

One gene, one mRNA, many proteins

Back to the whole journey: what's the same everywhere, and what's different in a cell without a nucleus.

ProkaryotesEukaryotes
WhereCytoplasm: transcription and translation togetherTranscription in the nucleus; translation in the cytosol or on the ER
RNA processingNone: the RNA is used right away5′ cap, poly(A) tail, splicing
Finding the startRibosome binding site just before AUGSmall subunit binds the 5′ cap and scans for AUG
ADDED FROM OUTSIDE THE COURSE MATERIALS

KEY TERMS
Start codon: the AUG where the ribosome starts. Coding region (open reading frame): from the start codon to the stop codon, including both. Template: for transcription, the DNA; for translation, the mRNA the ribosome reads.

RNA polymerase

THE COPIER
In the nucleus (eukaryotes) · in the cytoplasm (prokaryotes)
POWERBuilds RNA from a DNA template: reads the template 3′ → 5′, builds RNA 5′ → 3′. Needs no primer.

Spliceosome

In the nucleus of eukaryotes · RNA + protein
POWERCuts the introns out of a new mRNA and splices the exons together.

mRNA

THE MESSENGER
Made in the nucleus · read in the cytoplasm
POWERCarries one gene's instructions to the ribosome, written in codons, read 5′ → 3′. A 5′ cap and a poly(A) tail protect it.

tRNA

THE TRANSLATOR
In the cytoplasm · passes through the ribosome's A, P and E sites
POWERIts anticodon pairs with a codon (antiparallel); its 3′ end carries the matching amino acid.

Ribosome

THE ASSEMBLY LINE
Cytosol or the ER · 2 subunits, ~60% rRNA, ~40% protein
POWERReads the mRNA 5′ → 3′, one codon at a time, and joins amino acids with peptide bonds (made by its rRNA).
IF IT'S MISSINGNo peptide bonds: no proteins at all.

Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase

THE LOADER
In the cytoplasm · one for each amino acid
POWERAttaches the right amino acid to the right tRNA, using ATP. Doesn't need the mRNA.
IF IT'S MISSINGIts tRNAs stay empty: that amino acid can't be added to any chain.

Release factor

THE FINISHER
The ribosome's A site, at a stop codon
POWERBinds a stop codon in the A site and frees the finished polypeptide. A protein, not a tRNA.
IF IT'S MISSINGThe finished chain stays stuck to the last tRNA, and the ribosome can't let go.

Uracil (U)

PYRIMIDINE · 1 RING
In RNA only
