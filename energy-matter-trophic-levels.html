STEP 1 · START HERE

Two things move through every ecosystem

Every time one organism eats another, two things are passed along: energy and matter (the atoms themselves). They follow very different routes.

ENERGY · A ONE-WAY LINE

Energy flows

SunProducersConsumersHeat

Energy comes in as sunlight, climbs the trophic levels, and leaves as heat. It passes through once.

MATTER · A LOOP

Matter cycles

ProducersConsumersDecomposersNutrientsProducers again

The atoms are never used up. The same carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus go around again and again.

KEY TERMS · TAP ONE TO OPEN IT
Trophic level

A step in the feeding order of an ecosystem. Producers are level 1.

Primary producer

An organism that makes its own food, usually from sunlight. Also called an autotroph. Level 1.

Consumer

An organism that gets its energy by eating others. Also called a heterotroph. Primary consumers eat producers, secondary consumers eat primary consumers, tertiary consumers eat secondary ones.

Food web

All the feeding links in a community. Real communities are webs, not single chains.

Omnivore

In ecology, a consumer that feeds at more than one trophic level.

Decomposer

Bacteria, archaea and fungi that break dead matter down chemically and release its nutrients.

STEP 3 · THE RULES ENERGY FOLLOWS

Two laws of thermodynamics

Two physical laws explain everything in the rest of this page.

FIRST LAW

Energy is never created or destroyed

A producer does not create energy. It converts sunlight into chemical energy stored in sugars.

When energy moves up a level, the total is all still there. It is stored in the eater's body, left in its waste, or released as heat.

SunlightChemical energy in a leafBody + waste + heat
SECOND LAW

No energy transfer is 100% efficient

Each time energy moves from one trophic level to the next, part of it becomes heat.

Why? Every organism spends energy to stay alive. That is respiration. Once the energy has been used, it ends up as heat.

So each trophic level holds less energy than the one below it.

KEY TERMS · TAP ONE TO OPEN IT
First law of thermodynamics

Energy is never created or destroyed. It is only converted from one form to another or passed on.

Second law of thermodynamics

No energy transfer is 100% efficient. Some energy always ends up as heat.

STEP 4 · WHY SO LITTLE IS PASSED ON

Trophic efficiency

Follow 1,000 units of energy from the producers (level 1) into one primary consumer (level 2). It shrinks in three stages.

DIAGRAM · EXAMPLE NUMBERS, BAR LENGTH = ENERGY

1,000 units in, 100 units kept

1,000 available in the producers
500 eaten · consumption
The other 500 is never eaten.
250 absorbed · assimilation
The other 250 leaves as waste.
100 becomes new body mass · production
The other 150 is used to stay alive and ends as heat.

Only the last bar, the new body mass, is there for level 3 to eat.

THE MATH

Three efficiencies, multiplied

CE · CONSUMPTION EFFICIENCYeaten ÷ available = 500 ÷ 1,000 = 50%
AE · ASSIMILATION EFFICIENCYabsorbed ÷ eaten = 250 ÷ 500 = 50%
PE · PRODUCTION EFFICIENCYnew body mass ÷ absorbed = 100 ÷ 250 = 40%
TROPHIC EFFICIENCY = CE × AE × PE0.5 × 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.10, so 10% is passed on
Try one: producers make 5,000 units. CE = 20%, AE = 40%, PE = 25%.

0.20 × 0.40 × 0.25 = 0.02, a trophic efficiency of 2%. Production at level 2 = 5,000 × 0.02 = 100 units.

KEY TERMS · TAP ONE TO OPEN IT
Consumption efficiency (CE)

The share of the energy available at one level that the next level actually eats.

Assimilation efficiency (AE)

The share of what is eaten that is absorbed. The rest leaves as waste.

Production efficiency (PE)

The share of what is absorbed that becomes new body mass. The rest is used to stay alive and ends as heat.

Trophic efficiency

The share of one level's energy that becomes new body mass at the next level. CE × AE × PE, about 10% as a rule of thumb.

STEP 5 · THE RULE OF THUMB

About 10% moves up

The exact number changes from one ecosystem to another, but about 10% of the energy in one trophic level reaches the next. The other 90% is not eaten, is lost as waste, or is used to stay alive.

WHAT IT MEANS

Why food chains are short

By level 4, only about a thousandth of the energy the producers captured is left: 10 out of 10,000, or 0.1%.

There is not enough energy to support many more levels. That is why food chains rarely go past four or five steps, and why top predators are always rare.

WHERE IT ALL GOES

Why energy can't cycle

Sooner or later, all of that energy ends up as heat. Even waste and dead matter: decomposers use it, and it becomes heat too.

Heat can't be turned back into food energy. It is a one-way exit. So energy has to keep arriving from outside. It is transferred in from the sun, not created.

STEP 6 · THE OTHER JOURNEY

Matter goes around forever

Atoms like carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus are never used up. The same bite that passes on energy also passes on atoms, and the atoms get used again.

1Producers
Take up nutrients and build them into their bodies.
2Consumers
Get their atoms by eating. The atoms are built into the eater's body.
3Detritus
Everything that dies, and all waste, collects as dead matter.
4Detritivores and decomposers
Detritivores, like worms, eat and shred it. Decomposers (bacteria, archaea, fungi) digest it chemically.
5Nutrients released
Back into the soil, water and air, where producers can use them again. Return to 1.

Energy vs. matter

A LINE

Energy flows

COMES FROMOutside the ecosystem: the sun.
MOVES BYFeeding, up the trophic levels.
AT EACH STEPAbout 90% stays behind or is used to stay alive.
ENDS ASHeat, which can't be reused.
SOIt passes through once and must keep arriving.
A LOOP

Matter cycles

COMES FROMInside the system: pools in the air, soil, water and living things.
MOVES BYFeeding, then decomposition.
AT EACH STEPThe atoms are rebuilt into new molecules, never lost.
ENDS ASNutrients producers can take up again.
SOThe same atoms go around forever.

Lock it in

  1. A trophic level is a step in the feeding order. Producers are level 1. Arrows point from food to eater.
  2. Energy is never created or destroyed, only converted or passed on.
  3. Trophic efficiency = CE × AE × PE. As a rule of thumb, about 10% of a level's energy moves up.
  4. Energy flows one way and ends as heat. Matter cycles between pools.
  5. Detritivores eat dead matter. Decomposers turn it back into nutrients producers can use.
